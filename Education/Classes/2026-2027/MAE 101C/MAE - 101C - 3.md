# Radiation Exchange: Two Gray Surfaces

![[MAE - 101C - 3-1792140090174.webp|431]]Both surfaces emit and absorb. ${}\dot{Q}_{12}{}$ is the **net** exchange: what 1 sends to 2 minus what 2 sends to 1.

${}\mathcal{F} = \text{transfer factor}{}$

Depends on the emissivities and on the geometry: how much each surface "sees" of the other. 

$$
\boxed{\dot{Q}_{12} = A_{1}\mathcal{F}_{12}(\sigma T_{1}^{4}-\sigma T_{2}^{4})}

$$


# Special Case: Small Body in a Large Enclosure
![[MAE - 101C - 3-1792141006818.webp|364]]Assumptions
- Surface 1 is completely surrounded by surface 2
- ${}A_{1}\ll A_{2}{}$
- surface 2 is black

Then ${}\mathcal{F}=\varepsilon_{1}{}$ and:
$$
\boxed{\dot{Q}_{12}=A_{1}\varepsilon_{1}\sigma(T_{1}^{4}-T_{2}^{4})}
$$
Examples: a person in a room, a pipe in a large hall, a satellite facing deep space. The enclosure's ${}\varepsilon{}$ drops out!




${}\dot{Q}_{12} = A_{1}\varepsilon_{1}(\sigma T_{1}^{4}-\sigma T_{2}^{4}){}$  Net heat transfer

The ${}T^{4}{}$ dependance makes this complicated! What happens if ${}\underset{\underset{\text{order of magnitude}}{\downarrow}}{T_{1}\sim O(T_{2})}{}$ 
Example: 
${}\begin{matrix} 66\mathrm{{\degree C}} & 85\mathrm{{\degree C}}\end{matrix}{}$
${}\frac{T_{1}^{2}+T_{2}^{2}}{2}\cong T_{m}^{2}{}$
${}\frac{T_{1}+T_{2}}{2}\cong T_{m}{}$

$$
\begin{align}
\dot{Q}_{12}  & \cong  \varepsilon_{1}A_{1}\sigma(T_{1}^{4}-T_{2}^{4}) \\
 & = \varepsilon_{1}A_{1}\sigma(T_{1}^{2}+T_{2}^{2})(T_{1}^{2}-T_{2}^{2}) \\
 & = \varepsilon_{1}A_{1} \sigma(T_{1}^{2}+T_{2}^{2})(T_{1}+T_{2})(T_{1}-T_{2}) \\
\dot{Q}_{12}  &  = \varepsilon_{1}A_{1}\sigma(4T_{m}^{3})(T_{1}-T_{2}) \\
T_{m}  & \equiv \text{Mean of \(T_{1}\) and \(T_{2}\)}
\end{align}
$$
Another way to express this equation is to write it as:
$$
\boxed{\dot{Q}_{12}=A_{1}h_{r}(T_{1}-T_{2})}


$$
where ${}h_{r} = 4\varepsilon_{1}\sigma T_{m}^{3}{}$
${}h_{r} \equiv{}$Radiation heat transfer coefficient ${}\left[ \frac{\mathrm{{W}}}{\mathrm{{m}}^{2}\mathrm{{K}}} \right]{}$


# Linearizing Radiation 

The ${}T^{4}{}$ makes radiation nonlinear. If ${}T_{1}{}$ and ${}T_{2}{}$ are not too different, factor it:
$$
\begin{align}
T_{1}^{4}-T_{2}^{4} & = (T_{1}^{2}+T_{2}^{2})(T_{1}+T_{2})(T_{1}-T_{2}) \\
 & \approx   (2T_{m}^{2})(2T_{m})(T_{1}-T_{2})=4T_{m}^{3}(T_{1}-T_{2})
\end{align}

$$
**${}T_{m} ={}$mean of ${}T_{1}{}$ and ${}T_{2}{}$ (in kelvin)**

$$
\begin{matrix}
\boxed{\dot{Q}_{12}A_{1}h_{r}(T_{1}-T_{2})}   &  &  &  &  &  &  &  &  & &  &  &  \boxed{h_{r}=4\varepsilon_{1}\sigma T_{m}^{3}}
\end{matrix}
$$
 **radiation heat transfer coefficient ${}[\mathrm{{W}}/\mathrm{{m}}^{2}\cdot \mathrm{{K}}]{}$

# Example: Radiation Heat Loss from an Astronaut Suit
![[MAE - 101C - 3-1792143967372.webp|371x371]]
Astronaut suit outer-surface temperature: ${}T_{1}=\mathrm{{\degree C}}{}$ Research-lab surface temperature: ${}T_{2}=-100\mathrm{{\degree C}}{}$ Suit surface area: ${}A_{1}=3\mathrm{{m}}^{2}{}$ Suit: aluminized with ${}\varepsilon=0.05{}$ Find ${}Q_{12}{}$ (heat loss from the astronaut).


${}\sigma = 5.67\times 10^{-8}\mathrm{{W}}/\mathrm{{m}}^{2}\mathrm{{K}}^{4}{}$

Design goal: Don't want the astronaut to lose body heat!




$$
\begin{matrix}
T_{1} = 273\mathrm{{K}} &  & T_{2} = 173\mathrm{{K}} 
\end{matrix}
$$
$$

\begin{align}
\dot{Q}_{12}  & = \varepsilon_{1}A_{1}(\sigma T_{1}^{4}-T_{2}^{4}) \\
  & = 0.05\times 3\mathrm{{m}}^{2}\times 5.67\times 10^{-8}(273^{4}-173^{4}) \\
 \dot{Q}_{12} & \cong 39.6 \mathrm{{W}}
\end{align}
$$

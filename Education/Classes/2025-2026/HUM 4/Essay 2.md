### Professor Markman:
I have reviewed several outlines for Essay 2 and am encountering the same issue repeatedly.

Stating that the authors of the Women’s Movement, the documents of the Haitian Revolution, or Frederick Douglass extend Locke’s concepts of liberty or equality to women, people of color, or marginalized populations is **NOT a claim — and it is NOT EVEN A POSITION.** It is a statement of fact.

Please do not write in your thesis or topic sentences that these documents “take Locke’s concepts and apply them to new populations.” That identifies what the texts do at a surface level.

Instead, concentrate on **three subtopics**: one main topic and two subtopics. Then consider how the texts **reinterpret, transform, expose, complicate, or destabilize** Locke’s understanding of those concept. You have a list of possible subtopics in the lecture slides on Locke (copied below).  

**Here is a sample topic sentence:**  

The Haitian Declaration of Independence expands Locke’s concept of the right to resist by asserting that formerly enslaved people possess the same natural rights Locke attributes to free citizens.

**What’s wrong here?**

- There is no argument.
- There are two subtopics. “Right to resist” and “natural rights” are distinct subjects. The sentence drifts between them instead of developing one clearly.

  
**Now, what does a strong analytical topic sentence look like?**

_[textual anchor]_ By identifying French colonial institutions as the ongoing sources of Haitian oppression, _[position]_ the Haitian Declaration redefines Locke’s theory of right to resist tyranny as a moral imperative _[reasoning]_ because it treats tyranny not as only a political abuse of authority but as a violation of human dignity.

**When you have reasoning, you can anticipate a counterargument:**  

Actually, for Locke tyranny is more than political abuse because it violates natural rights….

**And, you can make a refutation that is meaning:**

Since Locke does not explicitly equate natural rights with morality, the violation of natural rights, though unjust, is not necessarily presented as a moral degradation as it is in the Haitian Declaration. For Locke, the right to resist therefore remains a political issue rather than a moral duty.

  
**Caution for Those Writing on the Women’s Movement**

The tendency to simply say that the documents extend rights to women is overwhelming in nearly every thesis and topic sentence I have read.

Do NOT fall into this trap.

If you find yourself writing only about “extending rights to women” without clearly defined conceptual subtopics, you may want to reconsider your focus or even reconsider your prompt. After all, I provided a very clear slide in yesterday’s lecture showing how the Haitian Declaration moves beyond Locke conceptually (see below). That is the level of argument you should be aiming for.

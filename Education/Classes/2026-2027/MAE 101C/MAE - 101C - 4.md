## Vertical wall with natural convection:

![[MAE - 101C - 4-1792232419395.webp]]

For gases, the location "transition" is determined by dimensionless number called the "Grashof Number" ${}G_{r}{}$: 
$$
\begin{matrix}
\boxed{G_{r} = \frac{\beta(\Delta T)gx^{3}}{\nu ^{2}}}   &   &  \begin{matrix}
\Delta T: T_{s}-T_{e} [\mathrm{K}]   \\
x \equiv \text{distance in } (\mathrm{m}) \\
g =~ \substack{\text{gravitational}\\ \text{acceleration}}~~ [\mathrm{m}/\mathrm{s}^{2}] \\
\beta =~ \substack{\text{Volumetric}\\ \text{expansion coeff.}} \\
\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho} [\mathrm{m}^{2}/\mathrm{s}]
\end{matrix}
\end{matrix}
$$
for ideal gas ${}\beta = \frac{1}{T} \left[ \frac{1}{\mathrm{K}} \right]{}$
for gases: ${}G_{r} \left|_{\text{transition}} \right. \simeq 10^{9}{}$

for air at normal temp. (in natural convection):

Laminar flow: ${}h_{c} = 1.07\left( \frac{\Delta T}{x} \right)^{1/4} {}$   ${}(10^{4}<G_{r,x}<10^{9}){}$
${}x \to {}$ at specific location

Turbulent flow: ${}h_{c} = 1.30 (\Delta T)^{1/3}{}$   ${}10^{9}<G_{r,x}<10^{12}{}$
	Unit: ${}h_{c} \left[ \frac{\mathrm{W}}{\mathrm{m}^{2}\mathrm{K}} \right]{}$   ${}\boxed{\dot{Q} = hA \Delta T}{}$
	
Note: ${}\left\{ \begin{matrix} \text{for laminar range } & \Rightarrow  & h_{c}\sim x^{-1/4} \\  \text{for turbulent range }  & \Rightarrow &  h_{c} \neq \text{for } (x) \simeq \text{const.}\end{matrix} \right.{}$

Most of the time, we are not interested in the variation of ${}h_{c}{}$ along the wall 
![[MAE - 101C - 4-1792234851258.webp|282]]We use ${}\overline{h_{c}} \equiv{}$Avg. heat transfer coeff.

$$
\dot{Q} = \overline{h_{c}}A(T_{s}-T_{e})
$$







![[MAE - 101C - 4-1792265961706.webp|303x300]]${}\overline{h_{c}}{}$ is obtained by the integration of ${}d\dot{Q}{}$ to find ${}\dot{Q}{}$ and then determine ${}\overline{h_{c}}{}$ (section 1.3.3 textbook)

$$
\begin{align}
d\dot{Q} &  = h_{c}(Wdx)(T_{s}-T_{e}) \\
\dot{Q} &  = \int_{0}^{L}h_{c}W(T_{s}-T_{e})dx \\
 & \quad \vdots \\
\end{align}
\\
\boxed{\overline{h_{c}}=\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{A}h_{c}dA}
$$
(Valid for both natural and forced convection)

## Summary & Combination 

We have seen 3 modes of heat transfer:

Combined Models of Heat Transfer:

${}\boxed{\text{CASE A}}{}$

Resistance to heat flow are in series:
![[MAE - 101C - 4-1792266423389.webp]]
$$
\begin{cases}
\text{Convection}:  & \dot{Q}=h_{c}A\Delta T & \text{(Newton's Law of Cooling)} \\
\text{Conduction}:  &  \dot{Q} = \frac{\kappa A}{L}\Delta T'  &  \text{(Fourier's Law)}
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{matrix}
\boxed{\dot{Q}_{\text{conv.}} = \frac{\Delta T}{\left( \frac{1}{h_{c}A} \right)}}  &   &   &  \boxed{\dot{Q}_{\text{cond.}} = \frac{\Delta T'}{\left( \frac{L}{\kappa A} \right)}} \\
①  &   &   &  ②
\end{matrix}
$$

But: ${}\dot{Q}={}$const. across each section under ${}\begin{cases}\text{steady state} & \frac{d}{dt}=0  \\  \dot{Q}_{\forall}= 0\end{cases}{}$

$$
\begin{align}
\dot{Q} = \frac{T_{i} - T_{1}}{\left( \frac{1}{h_{c,i}A} \right)} = \frac{T_{1}-T_{2}}{\left( \frac{L_{A}}{\kappa_{A}A} \right)}=\frac{T_{2}-T_{3}}{\left( \frac{L_{B}}{\kappa_{B}A} \right)}=\frac{T_{3}-T_{o}}{\left( \frac{1}{h_{c,o}A} \right)} \\
\end{align}

$$

$$
Comparison of eqs. 1 and 4

$$
\begin{gather}
\frac{1}{UA} = \frac{1}{h_{c,i}A} + \frac{L_{A}}{\kappa_{A}A} + \frac{L_{B}}{\kappa_{B}A}+\frac{1}{h_{c,o}A} \\
\boxed{\frac{1}{U}=\frac{1}{h_{c}}+\frac{L_{A}}{\kappa_{A}}+\frac{L_{B}}{\kappa_{B}}+\frac{1}{h_{c,o}}}

$$

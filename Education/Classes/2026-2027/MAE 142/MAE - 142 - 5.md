# Velocity

- Differentiate position vectors to find the velocity of point p.
![[MAE - 142 - 5-1793787289405.webp|359]]
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathbf{\vec{r}_{p}}^{E} = \mathbf{\vec{r}_{o}}^{E} + T_{L 2E}\mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L}\\
\dot{\mathbf{\vec{r}}}_{p}^{E} =\dot{\mathbf{\vec{r}}}_{o}^{E}+\dot{T}_{L2E} \mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L}+T_{L 2E} \dot{\mathbf{\vec{r}}}^{L}
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\dot{\mathbf{\vec{r}}}_{o}^{E}=0  & \begin{array}{l} \text{NED origin is not moving \\ relative to ECEF origin} \end{array} \\
\dot{T}_{L 2E}\mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L}\approx 0 & \begin{array}{l} \text{latitude and longitude \\ are changing very slowly} \end{array} 
\end{array}
$$




$$
\begin{array}{lll} 
\boxed{\dot{\mathbf{\vec{r}}}_{p}^{E}=T_{L 2E}\dot{\mathbf{\vec{r}}}^{L}}  & \text{or} & \boxed{\mathbf{\vec{v}}_{p}^{E}=T_{L 2E}\mathbf{\vec{v}}^{L}} & 
\end{array}
$$

# Velocity in ECEF Frame

- Differentiate ECEF position coordinates to find velocity in ECEF frame.

$$
\begin{array}{l}
X= (R_{E}+h)\cos \varphi \cos \lambda \\
Y =(R_{E}+h)\cos \varphi \sin \lambda  \\
Z = (R_{E}+h)\sin \varphi \\
\end{array}
\quad
\begin{array}{c}
\varphi = \text{latitude} \\
\lambda = \text{longitude} \\
h = \text{altitude (above Earth surface)} \\
R_{E} = \text{Earth radius}\approx 6367\,\mathrm{km}  
\end{array}
$$
$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{l}
\dot{X} = \dot{h}\cos \varphi \cos \lambda - (R_{E} + h)\dot{\varphi}\sin \varphi \cos \lambda - (R_{E}+h)\dot{\lambda}\cos \varphi \sin \lambda \\
\dot{Y} = \dot{h}\cos \varphi \sin \lambda - (R_{E}+h)\dot{\varphi}\sin \varphi \sin \lambda + (R_{E}+h)\dot{\lambda}\cos \varphi \cos \lambda \\
\dot{Z} = \dot{h}\sin \varphi+(R_{E}+h)\dot{\varphi}\cos \varphi
\end{array}}
$$

# Ground Relative Velocity

- It is assumed that the vehicle is navigating with a fluid (air or water or perhaps beer) and the fluid itself may be moving relative to the Earth. 
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\boxed{\mathbf{\vec{v}}_{v /g}= \mathbf{\vec{v}}_{v/f} + \mathbf{\vec{v}}_{f /g}} &  \begin{array}{l}
\small \text{vehicle speed relative to ground} \\
\small =\text{vehichle speed relative to fluid + fluid speed relative to ground}
\end{array}
\end{array}
$$
- The vehicle velocity over ground includes movement of the fluid.
$$
\begin{array}{lll} 
\boxed{\begin{array}{l} \\
V_{N}=V\cos \gamma \cos \chi + W_{N} \\ \\
V_{E} = V\cos \gamma \sin \chi+W_{E} \\ \\
V_{D}=-V\sin \gamma+W_{D} \\ \\
\end{array}}  
\begin{array}{c}
V=\text{airspeed} \\
\gamma = \text{flight path angle} \\
\chi = \text{flight path heading}  \\
 \\
V_{N } = \text{velocity north}  & W_{N} = \text{wind north}\\
V_{E} = \text{velocity east}  & W_{E} = \text{wind east}\\
V_{D} = \text{velocity down}  &  W_{D} = \text{wind down}
\end{array}
\end{array}
$$

# Trajectory Simulation

- The translational velocity equations are useful for simulating flight trajectories along planned routes or to illustrate the effect of winds.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\boxed{\begin{array}{l}
\dot{\varphi}=\frac{V\cos \gamma \cos \chi+W_{N}}{R_{E}+h} \\[2ex]
\dot{\lambda} = \frac{V\cos \gamma \cos \chi+W_{E}}{(R_{E}+h)\cos \varphi} \\[2ex]
\dot{h} = V\sin \gamma -W_{D}
\end{array}} & 
\tiny\begin{array}{c}
 \varphi = \text{latitude} \\
 \lambda = \text{longitude} \\
 h = \text{altitude (above Earth surface)} \\
  \\
 V = \text{airspeed} \\
 \gamma = \text{flight path angle} \\
 \chi  = \text{fligh path heading} \\
  \\
 W_{N} = \text{wind north} \\
 W_{E} = \text{wind east} \\
 W_{D} = \text{wind down} \\
  \\
 R_{E}\approx 6367\,\mathrm{km} 
\end{array}
\end{array}
$$
# Great Circles

- A great circle on a sphere is defined by the intersection of a plane passing though the center of the sphere.
- The shortest path between two point on a sphere is a great circle (called and orthodrome).
- The distance traveled on a great circle trajectory is given by the following equation:
$$
\boxed{d_{gc} = a\cos ^{-1}\{\sin \varphi_{A} \sin \varphi_{B}+\cos \varphi_{A}\cos \varphi_{B}\cos(\lambda_{B}-\lambda_{A})\}}
$$
![[MAE - 142 - 5-1794006565635.webp|183]]
$$\begin{array}{cc}
d_{gc} = \text{great circle path distance}  &  &  &  &  &  &  &  &&  \\
a = \text{semimajor axis} \\
 \\
(\varphi_{A},\lambda_{A}) = \text{latitude, longitude of start point} \\
(\varphi_{B}, \lambda_{B}) = \text{latitude, longitude of end point}
\end{array}
$$

# Rhumb Course

- A rhumb is a course on the Earth resulting from a constant heading.
- A rhumb course, called a loxodrome, is not generally a great circle (and therefore not the shortest route)
	- A great circle path has a continuously changing heading angle, making it difficult to follow.
- The distance traveled on a rhumb course is given by:
![[MAE - 142 - 5-1794007097831.webp|170]]
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\boxed{d_{rh}=a|\varphi_{B}-\varphi_{A}||\sec \chi|}  & \tau_{A}=\ln(\sec \varphi_{A} + \tan \varphi_{A})\\
\chi = \tan ^{-1}\left( \frac{\lambda_{B}-\lambda_{A}}{\tau_{B}-\tau_{A}} \right)  & \tau_{B} = \ln(\sec \varphi_{B}+\tan \varphi_{B})
\end{array}
$$

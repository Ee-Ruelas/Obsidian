## Thermal Energy VS Work

- By definition, electrical energy passing a boundary with no temperature difference driving it, ${}\Delta T{}$, is work not heat.
- Inside the resistor the energy converts to thermal energy, and is considered generation ${}Q_{\forall}{}$.

Key Idea: Heat is energy crossing a boundary because of a temperature difference (${}\Delta T \neq 0{}$)

For a *closed system* of *solid body*, the work done on the body is folded into ${}Q_{\forall}{}$. 
For a *open system* (pump, fan, turbine), the shaft work stays explicitly as ${}\dot{W}{}$.

## Nomenclature and Units

- ${}\dot{Q} = \text{ heat transfer rate \quad}[W]=\frac{J}{s}{}$
	- ${}\dot{Q} = \frac{Q}{t}{}$
- ${}q = \text{ heat flux \quad} [W/m^{2}]{}$
	- ${}q = \frac{\dot{Q}}{A}{}$
- ${}\dot{q} = \text{ volumetric heat source \quad } [W/m^{3}]{}$
	- ${}\dot{q}=\frac{\dot{Q}}{\forall}{}$

### Fourier's Law of Heat Conduction (Ch. 1.3.1) 

How do we connect heat with temperature gradients?

$$
 \frac{\dot{Q}}{A}  = q = -k \frac{dT}{dn} \quad [\mathrm{W/m^{2}}]
$$

${}q{}$ : heat flux = heat transfer rate per area ${}[\mathrm{W/m^{2}}]{}$
${} k:{}$thermal conductivity, ${}[\mathrm{\frac{W}{(mK)}}]{}$
${}n:{}$direction normal to the (isothermal) surface, points toward higher temperature ${}\Rightarrow{}$ negative sign!

**Thermal conductivity is temperature-dependent!**
![[MAE - 101C - 2-1791844822469.webp|294]]

This means that for a every material their conductivity changes as they become hotter or colder. 

![[MAE - 101C - 2-1791844917162.webp|275]]

Heat flux: ${}q = \frac{\dot{Q}}{A} \quad \left[ \mathrm{\frac{W}{m^{2}}} \right] \text{ or } \left[ \mathrm{\frac{J}{m^{2}s}} \right]{}$

$$
\begin{gather*}
q \alpha-\frac{dT}{dx} \Rightarrow q = -\kappa \frac{dT}{dx}\\
\\
\text{Fourier Law of Heat Conduction}
\end{gather*}
$$

From the energy equation for a system:

$$
\begin{gather}
\rho \forall c \frac{dT}{dt} = (\dot{Q}_{net}) + \dot{Q}_{\forall} \\
\end{gather}
$$
For this element: ${}\dot{Q}_{\forall} = 0 {}$. Also, assuming steady state ${}\frac{d}{dt}=0{}$.
$$
\begin{gather}
(\dot{Q}_{net})_{in}= 0 \Rightarrow \dot{Q}|_{x}-\dot{Q}|_{x+\Delta x} = 0 \\
\dot{Q}|_{x} = \dot{Q}|_{x+\Delta x} \equiv \dot{Q} = const \\
 \\
\text{Hence: } \dot{Q} = qA = \left( -\kappa  \frac{dT}{dx} \right)A \\
\text{Note: Rearrange and integrate:} \\
\frac{\dot{Q}}{A}dx = -\kappa  dT \Rightarrow \frac{\dot{Q}}{A}\int_{0}^{L}dx = -\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\kappa dT \\
\text{Assume } \kappa = \text{const.} \\
\frac{\dot{Q}L}{A} = -\kappa (T_{2}-T_{1}) \Rightarrow \dot{Q} = \frac{\kappa A}{L}(T_{1}-T_{2})
\end{gather}
$$

$$
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\dot{Q} = \frac{T_{1}-T_{2}}{\frac{L}{\kappa A}}}
\end{equation}
$$
Where ${}\frac{L}{\kappa A}{}$ is ${}R_{th}{}$: thermal resistance


Sign Check:  ${}T_{1}>T_{2}\Rightarrow dT/dx<0\Rightarrow 1>0:{}$heat flows in +${}x{}$, from hot to cold.

Preview:
- ${}R=\frac{L}{\kappa A}{}$
- ${}Q = \frac{\Delta T}{R}{}$: an "Ohm's Law" for heat

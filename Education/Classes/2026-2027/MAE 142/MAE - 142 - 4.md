## Satellite Navigation

- GPS satellite launched in 1989; became operational 1994
- Master control station sends GPS satellite constellation and corrects, periodically, each Space Vehicle (SV)
- SV complete 2 orbits per sidereal day
- About 32 active SV's

## GPS Measurements

- GPS uses a technique known as multilateration to estimate user position 

$$
\begin{matrix}
\boxed{\rho_{i} = c(t_{r } - t_{si})+\text{corrections}} &  & \begin{matrix}
\rho_{i} = \text{pseudo-range measurement from satellite i}\\
t_{r} = \text{time received at user}\\
t_{si}=\text{time sent from satellite i}\\
c=\text{peed of light}\\
c=299.792458×10^{6} \mathrm{{m/s}}
\end{matrix}
\end{matrix}
$$
- Pseudo-range measurements represent a biased distance measurement between the unknown user position and the known satellite position.

$$
\begin{matrix}
\boxed{\rho_{i}+b=||\vec{s}_{i}-\vec{x}||} & \begin{matrix}
\rho_{i} = \text{pseudo-range measurement from satellite i}\\
\vec{s}_{i}=\text{ECEF position of satellite i}\\
\vec{b} = \text{receiver clock bias}\\
\vec{x} = \text{user ECEF position}
\end{matrix}
\end{matrix}
\\
\\
||.|| = \text{Euclidian Distance}
$$

## Satellite Position

- Satellite position is obtained using ephemeris data that is included in the digital messages sent from the satellite to the GPS receiver.

- Mean Motion: ${}n = \sqrt{ \frac{\mu}{a^{3}} }{}$    ${}a = \left[ \frac{\mu}{n^{2}} \right]^{1/3}{}$      ${}\mu={}$gravitation constant

- Mean Anomaly: ${}M = M_{0}+ n(t-t_{0}){}$    ${}t_{0}={}$reference time that defines (${}\Omega_{0},M_{0}{}$)

- Eccentric Anomaly: ${}E = M+ e\sin E{}$
- True Anomaly: ${}\tan\left( \frac{\theta}{2} \right)=\sqrt{ \frac{1+e}{1-e} }\tan \left( \frac{E}{2} \right){}$   ${}\begin{matrix}\theta=\text{true anomaly}\\ a=\text{semi-major axis}\\ e=\text{eccentricity}\\ E=\text{eccentric anomaly}\\ M =\text{mean anomaly}\end{matrix}{}$


## Satellite Position in ECEF

- Argument of Latitude: ${}\phi = \theta+\omega{}$
- Argument of Longitude: ${}\Omega=\Omega_{0}+\omega_{e}(t-t_{0}){}$    ${}\omega_{e} = {}$Earth rotation rate
- Orbit Radius: ${}r=a(1-e\cos E)= \frac{a(1-e^{2})}{1+e\cos \theta}{}$

- The satellite position is then converted from the perifocal reference frame to Earth-Centered Earth-Fixed (ECEF) coordinates.
$$
\vec{s}^{ECEF} = \begin{bmatrix}
r\cos \phi \cos \Omega-r\sin \phi \sin \Omega \cos \iota \\
r\cos \phi \sin \Omega+r\sin \phi \cos \Omega \cos \iota \\
r\sin \phi \sin \iota
\end{bmatrix}

$$
${}\begin{matrix}\iota= \text{inclanation}  \\  \omega= \text{argument of perigee}\end{matrix}{}$

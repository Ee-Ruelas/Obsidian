# Accelerometers

- As long as the vehicle is not moving, and accelerometer measures the three body-axis components of the gravity vector.
	- The general case of a moving vehicle will be discussed in a later lecture.

$$
\begin{array}{c}
\mathbf{\vec{g}}^{B} = T_{L 2 B}\mathbf{\vec{g}}^{L} \\
\begin{bmatrix}
g_{x} \\
g_{y} \\
g_{z} 
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
\cos \theta \cos \psi & \cos \theta si\psi & -\sin \theta \\
\sin \phi \sin \theta \cos \psi-\cos \phi \sin \psi & \sin \phi \sin \theta \sin \psi+\cos \phi \cos \psi  & \sin \phi \cos \theta \\
\cos \phi \sin \theta \cos \psi+\sin \phi \sin \psi & \cos \phi \sin \theta \sin \psi-\sin \phi \cos \psi & \cos \phi \cos \theta
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
0  \\
0 \\
g
\end{bmatrix}
\end{array}
$$
- An accelerometer actually measures applied force, which means the direction of the gravity vector is reversed.
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
g_{x} \\
g_{y} \\
g_{z}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
-g\sin \theta \\
g\sin \phi \cos \theta \\
g\cos \phi \cos \theta 
\end{bmatrix}{\Huge\Rightarrow}
\boxed{\begin{bmatrix}
a_{x} \\
a_{y} \\
a_{z}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
g\sin \theta \\
-g\sin \phi \cos \theta \\
-g\cos \phi \cos \theta
\end{bmatrix}}
$$
# Roll and Pitch Euler Angles

- Use accelerometer measurements to find roll and pitch Euler angles.
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\begin{bmatrix}
a_{x} \\
a_{y} \\
a_{z}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
g\sin \theta \\
-g\sin \phi \cos \theta \\
-g\cos \phi \cos \theta
\end{bmatrix} 
  & \begin{array}{ll}
  a_{x}=g\sin \theta \\
  a_{y}^{2}+a_{z}^{2}=g^{2}\sin ^{2}\phi \cos ^{2}\theta+g^{2}\cos ^{2}\phi \cos ^{2}\theta =g^{2}\cos ^{2}\theta
  \end{array}
\end{array}

$$
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{ \theta \in[-\pi/2,\pi/2] }{ \boxed{\theta = \tan ^{-1}\left[ \frac{a_{x}}{\sqrt{ a_{y}^{2}+a_{z}^{2} }} \right]} }  & \boxed{\phi = \tan ^{-1}\left[ \frac{-a_{y}}{-a_{z}} \right]}
\end{array}

$$

# Solve for Heading Angle

- Break the product of L2B and L2M transformations into two parts.

$$
\begin{array}{llc}
\begin{bmatrix}
m_{x} \\
m_{y} \\
m_{z}
\end{bmatrix}=T_{L 2 B}T_{M 2 L}\begin{bmatrix}
M \\
0 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}  & {\Huge\Rightarrow}  & \begin{bmatrix}
m_{x} \\
m_{y} \\
m_{z}
\end{bmatrix}=T_{1}(\phi)T_{2}(\theta)T_{3}(\psi)T_{3}(-\delta)T_{2}(\iota)\begin{bmatrix}
M \\
0 \\
0
\end{bmatrix} \\
 &  &  {\Huge\Downarrow} \\
 &  &  T_{2}^{T}(\theta)T_{1}^{T}(\phi)\begin{bmatrix}
 m_{x} \\
 m_{y} \\
 m_{z}
 \end{bmatrix}=T_{3}(\psi)T_{3}(-\delta)T_{2}(\iota)\begin{bmatrix}
 M \\
 0 \\
 0
 \end{bmatrix}
\end{array}

$$
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
m_{x}\cos \theta+m_{y}\sin \theta \sin \phi+m_{z}\sin \theta\cos \phi &=M\cos \iota \cos(\psi-\delta)\\
m_{y}\cos \phi -m_{z}\sin \phi &= -M\cos \iota \sin(\psi-\delta)\\
-m_{x}\sin \theta+m_{y}\cos \theta \sin \phi + m_{z} \cos \theta \cos \phi &= M\sin \iota
\end{aligned}}
$$

# Attitude Determination Algorithm

- Vehicle attitude is determined using the following algorithm
	- This formulation is sometimes called a "tilt-compensated" compass.

Accelerometer measures: ax,ay,az
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
 \boxed{\phi = \tan ^{-1}\left[ \frac{-a_{y}}{-a_{z}} \right]}  &   & \underset{ \theta \in[-\pi /2, \pi /2] }{ \boxed{\theta=\tan ^{-1}\left[ \frac{a_{x}}{\sqrt{ a_{y}^{2}+a_{z}^{2} }} \right]} }
\end{array}
$$
Magnetometer measure: mx,my,mz
$$
\boxed{\psi=\delta+\tan ^{-1}\left[ \frac{m_{z}\sin \phi-m_{y}\cos \phi}{m_{x}\cos \theta+m_{y}\sin \theta \sin \phi+m_{z}\sin \theta \cos \phi} \right]}
$$


# Geospatial Position

- Earth-Centered Earth-Fixed (ECEF) Frame

## Earth-Centered, Earth-Fixed (ECEF) Frame

- ECEF (X,Y,Z) has its origin fixed to the center of the Earth.
- ECEF is fixed to the Earth and rotates with it.

${}\lambda{}$ is the angle from the Prime Meridian (x-axis)
${}\phi{}$ is the angle from the Equatorial Plane (z-axis)
![[MAE - 142 - 3-1792373242955.webp|386]]
## ECEF and Geodetic Conversions

- Convert from geodetic to ECEF (assuming spherical Earth):
$$
\begin{matrix}
\begin{matrix}
X = (R_{E}+h)\cos \phi \cos \lambda \\
Y = (R_{E}+h) \cos \phi \sin \lambda \\
Z = (R_{E}+h) \sin \phi
\end{matrix} 
  &  
\begin{matrix}
\phi = \text{latitude} \\
\lambda = \text{longitude} \\
h = \text{altitude (above Earth surface)} \\
\boxed{R_{E}= \text{Earth radius}\approx 6367\mathrm{{km}}}
\end{matrix} 
\end{matrix}
$$

- Convert from ECEF to geodetic:
$$
\begin{align}
 & \tan \phi = \frac{Z}{\sqrt{ X^{2} }+Y^{2}}  \\
 & h = \sqrt{ X^{2}+Y^{2}+Z^{2} }-R_{E}
\end{align}
\begin{matrix}
 \tan \lambda = \frac{Y}{X} \\
  \\
 
\end{matrix}

$$
## North-East-Down (NED) Frame

## Rotation ECEF to NED Frame

- The first two rotations have led to the intermediate frame (X',Y',Z')

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
X' \\
Y' \\
Z' \\
\end{bmatrix}=
T_{2}(-\phi)T_{3}(\lambda)
\begin{bmatrix}
X \\
Y \\
Z
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \phi  &  0  & \sin \phi \\
0  &  1  &  0 \\
-\sin \phi & 0  & \cos \phi
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \lambda  &  \sin \lambda  & 0  \\
-\sin \lambda &  \cos \lambda  &  0  \\
0  &  0  & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
X \\
Y \\
Z
\end{bmatrix}

$$
![[MAE - 142 - 3-1792374320628.webp|302]]But the intermediate frame is not quite aligned with NED frame

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
N \\
E \\
D 
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
-1 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
X' \\
Y' \\
Z'
\end{bmatrix}=T_{2}(-90\degree)\begin{bmatrix}
X' \\
Y' \\
Z'
\end{bmatrix}
$$







## Combine Rotations

- Combine all three rotations to yield the transformation matrix from ECEF to NED
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
N \\
E \\
D
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
-1 & 0 & 
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \phi  &  0  &  \sin \phi \\
0  &  1  & 0 \\
-\sin \phi & 0  & \cos \phi
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \lambda  & \sin \lambda & 0 \\
-\sin \lambda  & \cos \lambda  & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
X \\
Y \\
Z
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Note that the correct ordering must be maintained during multiplication!

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
N \\
E \\
D
\end{bmatrix} = T_{E 2L}\begin{bmatrix}
X \\
Y \\
Z
\end{bmatrix}
\quad\quad
\boxed{T_{E 2L}=\begin{bmatrix}
-\sin \phi \cos \lambda  &  - \sin \phi \sin \lambda  &  \cos \phi \\
-\sin \lambda  & \cos \lambda  & 0 \\
-\cos \phi \cos \lambda  & - \cos \phi \sin \lambda  & -\sin \phi
\end{bmatrix}}

$$

## Geospatial Position in ECEF

- The position of point 'p' can be expressed in ECEF coordinates.


![[MAE - 142 - 3-1792374768032.webp|340]]
$$
\begin{matrix}
\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{p}^{E}=\mathbf{\vec{r}_{o}}^{E} + \mathbf{\vec{r}}^{E}  & \mathbf{\vec{r}}^{E}=T_{L 2E}\mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L}
\end{matrix}
\\ \\
\boxed{\mathbf{\vec{r}_{p}}^{E}=\mathbf{\vec{r}_{o}}^{E}+T_{L 2E}\mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L}}\\
\Downarrow
$$

![[MAE - 142 - 3-1792375198738.webp]]

## Geospatial Position in Local NED

- The orthogonality property of the rotation matrix makes it easy to find the NED position in terms of the local reference and ECEF position

$$
\begin{align}
 & \mathbf{\vec{r}_{p}}^{E} = \mathbf{\vec{r}_{o}}^{E} + T_{L 2E}\mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L}
\\
 & \mathbf{\vec{r}_{p}}^{E}-\mathbf{\vec{r}_{o}}^{E}=T_{L 2E}\mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L}
\\
 & T_{L 2E}^{T}(\mathbf{\vec{r}_{p}}^{E}-\mathbf{\vec{r}_{o}}^{E}) = T^{T}_{L 2E}T_{L 2E}\mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L} \quad\text{but} \quad T_{L2E}^{T}T_{L 2E} = I
\end{align}
$$
$$
\boxed{\mathbf{\vec{r}}^{L}=T^{T}_{L 2E}(\mathbf{\vec{r}_{p}}^{E} - \mathbf{\vec{r}_{o}}^{E}) = T_{E 2L}(\mathbf{\vec{r}_{p}}^{E}- \mathbf{\vec{r}_{o}}^{E})}
$$

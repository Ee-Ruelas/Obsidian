# Center of Gravity

- The vehicle center-of-gravity is the point where the entire body can be balanced in a static equilibrium.

![[MAE - 142 - 11-1794537790108.webp|341]]
$$
\begin{array}{l}
\mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}=\text{weight vector element }i \\
0=\displaystyle\sum \mathbf{\vec{F}}_{cg}=\mathbf{\vec{R}}-\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i} \\
0=\displaystyle\sum \mathbf{\vec{M}}_{cg}=\sum_{i}(\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{i}-\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{cg})\times \mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}
\end{array}
$$





$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{ \text{resultant force} }{ \boxed{\mathbf{\vec{R}}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}} }  & &  &  \underset{ \text{center-of-gravity location} }{ \boxed{\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{cg}\times \mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{i}\times \mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}} }
\end{array}
$$


# Center of Mass

- The center of mass assumes all weight vectors act in the same direction.

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}=m_{i}\mathbf{\vec{g}}}\\
\begin{array}{llc}
\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{cg}\times \mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{i}\times \mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i} & \longrightarrow & \displaystyle\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{cg}\times m_{i}\mathbf{\vec{g}}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{i}\times m_{i}\mathbf{\vec{g}} \\
 &  & \downarrow \\
 \displaystyle\mathbf{\vec{R}}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}=\sum_{i}m_{i}\mathbf{\vec{g}}  &  & \displaystyle \left[ \sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{cg}m_{i} \right]\times \mathbf{\vec{g}}=\left[ \sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{i}m_{i} \right]\times \mathbf{\vec{g}} \\
\end{array}
\\
 \begin{array}{lll} 
  \displaystyle\boxed{M=\sum_{i}m_{i}}  & \boxed{\mathbf{\vec{R}}=M\mathbf{\vec{g}}} & \boxed{\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{cg}=\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{cm}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i}m_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{i}}
 \end{array}
$$

# Center of Buoyancy

- Archimedes says that when a body is submerged in a fluid, there is an upward buoyancy force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the body.
$$
\begin{array}{cll}
\boxed{\mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}=-\rho \nabla_{i}\mathbf{\vec{g}}}  &  \rlap{\begin{array}{c}
\rho=\text{fluid density} \\
\nabla_{i} = \text{volume of element }i
\end{array}} \\
\displaystyle\mathbf{\vec{R}}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{\vec{w}}_{i}=-\rho \sum_{i}\nabla_{i}\mathbf{\vec{g}}=-\rho \nabla \mathbf{\vec{g}}  \\
\begin{array}{ccc}
\boxed{\nabla=\sum_{i}\nabla_{i}} & \boxed{\mathbf{\vec{R}}=\rho \nabla \mathbf{\vec{g}}} & \boxed{\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{cb}=\frac{1}{\nabla}\sum_{i}\nabla_{i}\mathbf{\vec{r}}_{i}}
\end{array}
\end{array}
$$
The center of buoyancy is located at the volume centroid of the body.


# Moments of Inertia

- The moment of inertia matrix can be estimated from the vehicle mass distribution.

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
I = \begin{bmatrix}
I_{xx} & -I_{xy} & -I_{xz} \\
-I_{yz} & I_{yy} & -I_{yz} \\
-I_{zx} & -I_{zy} & I_{zz} 
\end{bmatrix} & \mathbf{\vec{r}}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}
x_{i} \\
y_{i} \\
z_{i} \\
\end{bmatrix} & \begin{array}{l}
\text{location of mass\\element within a body-\\fixed reference frame}
\end{array} \\
\end{array}
$$
polar moments of inertia
$$
\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}}
I_{xx} = \sum_{i}m_{i}(y_{i}^{2}+z_{i}^{2}) & &  I_{yy}=\sum_{i}m_{i}(z_{i}^{2}+x_{i}^{2}) & &  I_{zz} = \sum_{i}m_{i}(x_{i}^{2}+y_{i}^{2})
\end{array}
$$
products of inertia
$$
\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l}
I_{xy}=I_{yx}=\sum_{i}m_{i}x_{i}y_{i} &  & I_{yz}=I_{zy}=\sum_{i}m_{i}y_{i}z_{i} &  & I_{zx}=I_{xz}=\sum_{i}m_{i}z_{i}x_{i}
\end{array}

$$

# Wind-to-Body Axis Transformation

- Two rotations are needed to transform from the wind axis into the body-fixed axis.
	- Rotate about negative z-axis (3-axis) by the sideslip angle.
	- Rotate about positive y-axis (2-axis) by the angle-of-attack.

$$
T_{W 2 B} = \begin{bmatrix}
\cos \alpha & 0 & -\sin \alpha \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
\sin \alpha & 0 & \cos \alpha
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \beta & -\sin \beta & 0 \\
\sin \beta & \cos \beta & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}\\ \\
T_{W 2 B}=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \alpha \cos \beta & -\cos \alpha \sin \beta & -\sin \alpha \\
\sin \beta & \cos \beta & 0 \\
\sin \alpha \cos \beta & -\sin \alpha \sin \beta & \cos \alpha
\end{bmatrix}
$$

# Formulas for Angle-of-Attack and Sideslip

- Transforming the airspeed (vector) into body-axis coordinates establishes relationships involving angle-of-attack and sideslip.

$$
\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}c>{\displaystyle}r}
\mathbf{\vec{v}}^{B}=T_{W 2 B}\mathbf{\vec{v}}^{W} & {\Huge\Rightarrow} & \begin{bmatrix}
u \\
v \\
w 
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \alpha \cos \beta & -\cos \alpha \sin \beta & -\sin \alpha \\
\sin \beta & \cos \beta & 0 \\
\sin \alpha \cos \beta & -\sin \alpha \sin \beta & \cos \alpha
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
V \\
0 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}
\end{array}
$$
$$
\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l}
\boxed{\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l}
u=V\cos \alpha \cos \beta \\
v=V\sin \beta \\
w=V\sin \alpha \cos \beta
\end{array}} &  & \boxed{
\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l}
V=\sqrt{ u^{2}+v^{2}+w^{2} } \\
\alpha=\tan ^{-1}\left( \frac{w}{u} \right) \\
\beta=\tan ^{-1}\left( \frac{v}{\sqrt{ u^{2}+w^{2} }} \right)
\end{array}}
\end{array}
$$

# Aerodynamic Coefficients

- Aerodynamic force and moments are normalized to a non-dimensional form.
- The resulting non-dimensional coefficients can be compared with other airplanes.

$$
\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l}
\mathbf{\vec{F}}^{B}=\begin{bmatrix}
F_{x} \\
F_{y} \\
F_{z}
\end{bmatrix}=\bar{q}S\begin{bmatrix}
C_{x} \\
C_{y} \\
C_{z}
\end{bmatrix} &  & \mathbf{\vec{G}}^{B}=\begin{bmatrix}
L \\
M \\
N
\end{bmatrix}=\bar{q}S\begin{bmatrix}
bC_{l} \\
\bar{c}C_{m} \\
bC_{n}
\end{bmatrix}  &  \begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}c}
b=\text{wingspan} \\
\bar{c}=\text{mean geometric  chord} \\
S=\text{reference are} \\
 \\
\bar{q}=\frac{1}{2}\rho V^{2}=\text{dynamic pressure} \\
 \\
 \rho=\text{density} \\
 V=\text{airspeed}
\end{array}\\ \\
\mathbf{\vec{F}}^{B}=\begin{bmatrix}
F_{x} \\
F_{y} \\
F_{z}
\end{bmatrix}=\bar{q}ST_{W 2 B}\begin{bmatrix}
-C_{D} \\
-C_{C} \\
-C_{L}
\end{bmatrix} &  & \begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}c}
C_{D}=\text{drag coefficient} \\
C_{L} = \text{lift coefficient}
\end{array} 
\end{array}
$$

# Aerodynamic Data Format

- Each aerodynamic coefficient may depend upon:
	- Air-relative velocity (V or M, alpha, beta)
	- Rotational rates (p, q, r)
	- Configuration changes (control surface deflections, landing gear,...)

- Aerodynamic data is usually presented in the form of tabular data.
	- Data is collected from wind tunnel tests or computational modeling.
	- Simulation models use table "look-up" functions to extract aerodynamic data from tables.

$$
\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l}
\boxed{C_{L}=C_{L}(\alpha,\beta,V,p',q',r',\delta_{e},\delta_{a},\delta_{r},\dots)} & \begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}c}
\delta_{e}=\text{elevator deflection} \\
\delta_{a} =\text{aileron deflection} \\
\delta_{r}=\text{rudder deflection}
\end{array} \\
\begin{array}{>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l>{\displaystyle}l}
p'=\frac{pb}{2V} & q'=\frac{q\bar{c}}{2V} & r'=\frac{rb}{2V}
\end{array}
\end{array}
$$
